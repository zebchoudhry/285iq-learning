// AQA GCSE Biology 8461, topic 7: Ecology. Written for 285IQ.
// The three ecology lessons are broad, so each has ten questions.
topic: Ecology

lesson: Ecology - Lesson 1
MC | 1 | What is an ecosystem? | All the living organisms and the non-living parts of the environment in an area | All the plants and animals of a single species that live in an area | Only the living plants and animals that interact with each other in an area | The place where a group of organisms lives, feeds and finds shelter | An ecosystem includes the community of living things and their physical surroundings.
MC | 1 | Which of these is a biotic factor? | The number of predators | Temperature | Light intensity | Soil pH | Biotic factors are living things that affect an organism, such as predators, food supply, competition and disease.
MC | 1 | Which of these is an abiotic factor? | Light intensity | Availability of food | New predators | Competition for mates | Abiotic factors are non-living, such as light, temperature, moisture, wind speed and pH.
MC | 2 | What is a population? | All the organisms of one species living in an area | All the organisms of every species in an area | All the living and non-living things in an area | The place where an organism lives | A population is a group of the same species in one place.
MC | 2 | What is a community? | All the different species living in the same area | All the organisms of one species living in the same area | The non-living parts of an area, such as soil and water | A single organism and the place where it lives | A community is the populations of all the different species in an ecosystem.
MC | 2 | What is a habitat? | The place where an organism lives | The food an organism eats | The number of organisms in an area | The role of an organism | A habitat is the local environment of a species, such as a pond or a woodland floor.
MC | 2 | What do animals often compete with each other for? | Food, mates and territory | Light and carbon dioxide | Minerals from the soil | Water from the soil | Animals compete for food, water, mates and territory. Plants compete for light, water, space and minerals.
MC | 2 | What do plants often compete with each other for? | Light, water, space and minerals | Mates, territory and food | Food, shelter and prey | Oxygen, carbon dioxide and mates | Plants cannot move, so they compete for the resources they need to photosynthesise and grow.
MC | 3 | Why can a decrease in the number of predators cause the population of the prey to rise? | Fewer prey are eaten | The prey stop reproducing | The prey lose their food | The prey become predators | With fewer predators, fewer prey are killed, so their numbers can increase.
MC | 3 | What is interdependence? | Every species in a community depends on other species for food, shelter or pollination | A species depends only on the soil in which it grows or lives | Each species in a community can survive without any other species | The environment in a community stays the same however species change | A change to one species in a community can affect many others.
SA | 1 | What name is given to all the organisms of one species living in an area? (one word) | population | A population is the number of one species in an area.
SA | 2 | What name is given to factors in an ecosystem that are non-living? (one word) | abiotic | Light, temperature and moisture are abiotic factors.

lesson: Ecology - Lesson 2
MC | 1 | What piece of equipment is used to sample the number of plants in a small area? | A quadrat | A thermometer | A transect | A microscope | A quadrat is a square frame placed on the ground to count the organisms inside.
MC | 2 | What is a transect used for? | To see how the distribution of organisms changes across an area | To measure the temperature at different times of the day | To count the number of animals living in a pond or stream | To measure the pH of the soil at one place in a field | A line is laid across an area and organisms are sampled at regular intervals along it.
MC | 2 | Why should quadrats be placed randomly when sampling a field? | To avoid bias | To make the sample faster | To make the sample larger | To count all the organisms | Random placement gives a sample that represents the whole area.
MC | 2 | A student counts 12 daisies in 4 quadrats. What is the mean number of daisies per quadrat? | 3 | 4 | 12 | 48 | The mean is the total divided by the number of quadrats: 12 ÷ 4 = 3.
MC | 3 | A field has an area of 200 m². The mean number of daisies is 5 per m². Estimate the population of daisies in the field. | 1000 | 40 | 205 | 100 | Population size = mean number per m² × total area = 5 × 200 = 1000.
MC | 2 | Which abiotic factor would a student measure with a light meter? | Light intensity | Temperature | Soil pH | Wind speed | A light meter measures light intensity.
MC | 2 | Why is a large sample size better in a field investigation? | It gives more reliable results | It makes the results less accurate | It is quicker | It uses fewer quadrats | More samples reduce the effect of chance.
MC | 2 | Which tool would a student use to measure the pH of the soil? | A pH meter or indicator | A quadrat | A tape measure | A light meter | Soil pH is an abiotic factor measured with a pH probe or indicator.
MC | 3 | Fewer plants grow close to a large tree than far from it. Which abiotic factor is the most likely cause? | Light intensity | Soil colour | The shape of the leaves | The number of seeds | The tree shades the ground, so less light reaches the plants beneath it.
MC | 2 | A student wants to find out how the number of buttercups changes from the edge of a path into a field. Which method should they use? | A belt transect with quadrats at regular intervals | One quadrat placed in a random place in the field | Counting every buttercup in the whole field | Measuring only the temperature along the path | A transect shows a change in distribution along a gradient.
SA | 1 | What is the name of the square frame used to count organisms in a small area? (one word) | quadrat | Quadrats are placed on the ground and the organisms inside are counted.
SA | 2 | What name is given to a line along which samples are taken to show how organisms are distributed? (one word) | transect | A transect shows how a habitat changes with distance.

lesson: Ecology - Lesson 3
MC | 1 | What do arrows in a food chain show? | The direction in which energy is transferred | The direction in which the animals move | Which animal in the chain is the largest | Which animal in the chain is the fastest | Arrows point from the organism that is eaten to the organism that eats it.
MC | 1 | What is a producer? | An organism that makes its own food by photosynthesis | An organism that eats plants to get its energy | An organism that eats other animals to get its energy | An organism that breaks down dead matter for food | Producers, such as plants and algae, are at the start of a food chain.
MC | 2 | What is a primary consumer? | An animal that eats producers | An animal that eats other animals | A plant | A decomposer | Primary consumers are herbivores.
MC | 2 | What is a secondary consumer? | A carnivore that eats primary consumers | A herbivore that eats producers | A producer that makes its own food | A decomposer that breaks down dead matter | Secondary consumers eat primary consumers.
MC | 2 | What is an apex predator? | A predator with no natural predators of its own | The smallest predator in a food web | A herbivore at the start of the food chain | A producer that makes its own food | An apex predator is at the top of the food chain.
MC | 2 | In the food chain grass → rabbit → fox, which organism is the primary consumer? | Rabbit | Grass | Fox | There is none | The rabbit eats the grass, which is the producer.
MC | 2 | Why are food webs more realistic than food chains? | Most organisms eat more than one type of food and are eaten by more than one predator | They contain fewer organisms so they are much easier to study | They show only the plants that are found in an ecosystem | They show how populations in an ecosystem never change over time | A food web shows several linked food chains.
MC | 3 | In a food web, what is likely to happen to the number of rabbits if all the foxes are removed? | It increases at first | It decreases | It stays the same | It becomes zero | With no predators, fewer rabbits are eaten, so their numbers can rise.
MC | 3 | Why are there rarely more than five trophic levels in a food chain? | Energy is lost at each stage, so little is left for higher levels | Animals at higher levels are too small to be eaten | There is no light for plants at higher levels | Plants at the start are too large for animals to eat | Energy is lost as heat, in waste and in parts that are not eaten.
MC | 2 | What is the role of decomposers? | They break down dead plants and animals and return nutrients to the soil | They make food from carbon dioxide and water using light | They eat only live animals at the top of food chains | They carry pollen from one flower to another | Decomposers, such as bacteria and fungi, recycle nutrients.
SA | 1 | What name is given to an organism that makes its own food by photosynthesis? (one word) | producer | Producers are the first organism in a food chain.
SA | 2 | What name is given to the animals that eat producers? (two words) | primary consumers | Primary consumers are herbivores.
